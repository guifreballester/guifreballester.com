## TL;DR

I spotted a gap competitors missed in the post-COVID video market and built Twilio Live from 0→1. It reached ~$200K MRR for Twilio with 100+ customers.

## Context

Post-COVID, everyone needed video. But competitors focused on meetings and conferencing—Zoom, Teams, Google Meet. I saw an unserved need: streaming at scale for broadcasts, events, and one-to-many use cases.

## Problem

- Competitors focused on meetings (many-to-many), not streaming (one-to-many)
- Enterprises needed to broadcast to thousands, not host meetings
- Existing solutions required stitching together multiple tools
- No developer-friendly streaming API existed

## What I Did

1. **Customer discovery**: I ran extensive interviews to validate the streaming gap
2. **Business case**: I built the ROI models that justified the new product investment
3. **Product development**: I owned feature prioritization and the roadmap
4. **Sales enablement**: I created the technical sales materials and trained the team

## Key Decisions

### Find Gaps, Not Competition
Instead of competing head-to-head on meetings, I went after the underserved streaming market.

### Customer-Validated Features
Every feature started with customer validation. I built features that customers committed to paying for before development.

### Enterprise Focus
I concentrated on the enterprise segment where Twilio had real advantages—reliability, compliance, and scale.

### API-First Design
I kept Twilio's API-first philosophy, so Twilio Live integrated seamlessly with the rest of the Twilio platform.

## Technical Details

- **WebRTC architecture**: Selective Forwarding Unit (SFU) for scalable video
- **Video streaming**: Low-latency HLS for broadcasting to thousands
- **SDK design**: Native SDKs for iOS, Android, and JavaScript with consistent APIs
- **API documentation**: Interactive API explorer and comprehensive guides

## Lessons Learned

1. **Look for gaps, not competition**: Instead of fighting head-to-head, find the underserved market.
2. **Customer validation de-risks features**: Features built with customer commitment had much higher adoption.
3. **Enterprise sales needs PM support**: Technical content and training directly moved deal velocity.
4. **API consistency matters**: Developers expect products to work together. Design for the ecosystem.
